Questions, answered

How long does training actually take?

Pigeonhole samples the most recent ~50 messages from each of your folders using Microsoft's batched API, so a mailbox with 100 folders trains from roughly 5,000 messages — usually 30–90 seconds, dominated by download time. Building the model itself takes milliseconds. Hard cap: nowhere near 10 minutes.

Is my email really not uploaded anywhere?

Really. The add-in talks to exactly one service: Microsoft Graph, the same API Outlook itself uses, over your own Microsoft sign-in. Sender and subject metadata are converted to word counts in memory and stored as a small model in local storage on your device. Pigeonhole has no backend. You can verify this with your browser's network inspector — or read the source.

How is this different from Outlook rules?

Rules are exact-match and manual: you maintain them sender by sender, and they do nothing for the long tail of new senders. Pigeonhole ranks every folder for every message, including ones from senders it has never seen, using subjects, domains, and recipients. And when a sender should be a rule, the bolt button creates it in one click.

How is this different from AI email assistants?

They send your mail to a large language model in the cloud and pay per token — so you pay per token, and your mail is in someone else's datacenter. Folder prediction doesn't need a trillion parameters; it needs your own filing history. Pigeonhole's classifier is small enough to run locally, instant, free to run, and private by construction.

Which Outlook versions are supported?

Classic Outlook for Windows (Microsoft 365), the new Outlook for Windows, and Outlook on the web, with a Microsoft 365 / Exchange Online mailbox. The pane can be pinned so suggestions update as you move through your inbox. Gmail is on the roadmap.

What happens when the trial ends?

Filing pauses and your model stays put. Enter a license key ($30/year) and you're back exactly where you left off. Nothing is deleted.
